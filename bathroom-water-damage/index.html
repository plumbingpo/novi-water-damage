Bathroom Water Damage Help in Novi

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. In Novi, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet area without tearing out more than we need to, then open only the sections that cannot be dried in place.

Signs to Look For

  • Soft, springy flooring alongside the tub or where you stand at the vanity
  • The ceiling beneath the bathroom shows a brownish ring or has started to droop
  • Floor tiles that sound hollow, rock underfoot, or have come loose near the tub
  • Slight rocking when you sit on the toilet, or a dark ring on the floor around it
  • Grout lines that stay dark and damp long after the last shower
  • The caulk line at the shower or tub has split, pulled away, or turned black

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Leaking Shower Pan

A cracked pan or liner lets water out under the shower floor every time it is used. Up top everything looks fine, yet the framing underneath never dries.

The Room Below

The stain on the lower ceiling can be several feet off from the leak itself, because water travels along pipes and joists first. Light fixtures and wiring can be in its path.

Rotted Floor at the Toilet

When the wax ring gives out, a bit of water escapes every time the toilet is flushed. It is category 3 water, and the wood surrounding the flange decays until the toilet wobbles.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

First days

Water Slips Through

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months or more

Structure Weakens

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

What We Do

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Pinpoint the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Soft wall board, loose tile and subfloor that has rotted are removed. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.
