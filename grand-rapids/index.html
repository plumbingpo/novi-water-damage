How Drying Works on a Grand Rapids Water Damage Job

Drying a building is not the same as waiting for it to dry. Left alone, water trapped in drywall, wood framing and subfloor can stay for weeks, which is long enough for mold and warping. Our job on every Grand Rapids property is to speed that up in a controlled way.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Then air movers are set along the walls to push a fast stream of air across wet surfaces and lift moisture out of them. Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air and send it down a drain, which keeps the room dry enough for evaporation to continue. Without the dehumidifiers, the air movers would just move damp air around.

A technician measures the wet materials daily against readings from an unaffected area of the property. For most Grand Rapids homes the target is met in 3 to 5 days, though hardwood, plaster and other dense materials need more time. The equipment comes out when the numbers say so, not when the calendar does.

Common Water Damage Issues in Grand Rapids

Water usually escapes at the same few vulnerable spots in a home. The list below covers what we are asked to clean up most for Grand Rapids owners.

Water heaters: Over the years, mineral deposits settle in a tank and corrode the metal underneath. The first sign is often a rusty puddle or a damp ring on the floor around the base. A split tank does not stop at one tankful, because the inlet keeps feeding it until the valve is shut. Heaters in a hallway closet or attic can ruin flooring and ceilings well beyond the utility area.

Roofs: Wind can lift shingles and drive rain underneath them in a single storm. Skylights, plumbing vents and chimneys are other entry points once their seals crack with age. Water travels along rafters before it drips, so the wet spot inside may be far from the hole outside. For roof leaks in Grand Rapids, we tarp where needed, pull out soaked insulation and dry the structure so the ceiling can be repaired.

Freezing: Depending on the season, a hard overnight freeze can burst lines that were fine the day before. Outdoor spigots, sprinkler feeds and pipes in crawl spaces or along outside walls are hit first. A house left empty or a wing kept unheated faces the greatest risk. No flow or a bare trickle at the tap on a frigid day means a frozen line, so close the main and get help.

Backups: A drain that gurgles or several fixtures that slow down together point to a blockage in the main line. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. The water is unsanitary, and hard surfaces need disinfecting after the solids and liquid are removed. Stay out of the affected rooms and avoid running water until the line is cleared.

Seepage: Dampness at the base of a basement or foundation wall often means water is collecting outside. The usual reasons are grading that tilts inward, clogged perimeter drains and downspouts that empty too close. Early clues include powdery white residue on the concrete, flaking wall paint and air that always smells musty. The team at Novi Restoration can extract standing water and dry the space, and we will tell you what we think let it in.

What to Do Before Our Grand Rapids Crew Arrives

The hour between finding water and the crew arriving is when a homeowner in Grand Rapids can do the most good. Safety comes before saving anything. If water is near outlets, cords or the electrical panel, stay out of it, and switch off power to that area only if you can reach the breaker without stepping in water. Water that came up from a drain or sewer line should be left completely alone.

Then shut the water off. Use the small valve behind the toilet or under the sink, or go straight to the main. Then move what you can carry: rugs, electronics, papers, and small furniture go to a dry room. Slide plastic or aluminum foil beneath the feet of anything too heavy to move, which stops stains and swelling.

Before mopping, record the damage on your phone, since an insurance company will ask what it looked like at the start. Mop or towel up small amounts of clean water if it is safe, and leave the rest to the crew heading to Grand Rapids.
